# Lesson 11 worksheet — sites, areas and exchanges

Use [the reading](lesson_11_geometry.md) and keep the [answer guide](../instructor/lesson_11_guide.md) closed. All atoms and boundaries are invented. A calculator is enough; show units and whether a number is per boundary or per **cell**. Occupancies are supplied trials, not minimized values.

## Meeting 26 — one fixed geometry

**A1 — worked inventory.** Copy the reading's two-boundary sketch: $N_b=8000$, $S=100$ sites and $A=20$ nm² per boundary, $x_b=0.10$, $\theta=0.25$. Make a bulk/boundary/whole-cell table for A atoms, B atoms and total atoms. Explain why the cell-average B fraction, $x_b$, and $\theta$ differ.

**A2 — guided excess.** For A1, calculate B reference count on all cell sites, excess B count, total interface area, atoms/nm² and mol/m². Repeat by the boundary-site-density route. Which result doubles if someone divides the whole-cell excess by $A$ instead of $2A$? What does $50/(2A)$ measure?

**A3 — basis diagnostic.** A colleague multiplies a bulk $\mu_B$ in J/mol by “20 exchanged B atoms” and reports joules. Identify the missing factor, state the mol amount, and explain why a fixed-site substitution also moves A the opposite way. Do not choose a numerical $\mu$.

**A4 — independent fresh geometry.** Another synthetic cell has $N_b=920$, two equivalent boundaries with $S=40$ sites and $A=10$ nm² **each**, $x_b=0.25$, $\theta=0.50$. Find A/B counts in each region and the whole cell. Calculate $\Gamma_B$ in atoms/nm² and mol/m² by both formulas. Name one physical assumption needed to call those two interfaces equivalent.

## Meeting 27 — open versus closed

**B1 — worked permitted exchange.** Return to A1. Boundary occupancy changes from 0.25 to 0.35. For an **open** fixed-$x_b$ reservoir, list the cell and reservoir changes in A and B counts. Show that the combined counts are unchanged. Why is a B-only addition invalid?

**B2 — guided closed sample.** Make the **same change** in a closed cell whose starting totals are 7350 A and 850 B. Find new boundary and bulk A/B counts and $x_b$. Show both total balances. What false B total would follow if $x_b$ were kept at 0.10?

**B3 — incompatible geometry.** A later sketch has one boundary with $A_1=10$ nm², $S_1=40$, $\theta_1=0.50$ and another with $A_2=15$ nm², $S_2=60$, $\theta_2=0.40$, both referred to $x_b=0.25$. Can the equivalent-boundary $2A$ and one-$\theta$ formula be reused? Compute the *whole-cell* excess atoms and divide by the actual total area. State what else must be aligned before comparing their free energies as alternative structural states.

**B4 — independent exit.** Using the fresh A4 cell with fixed total B=270, let boundary occupancy rise from 0.50 to 0.625. Find the new bulk B fraction, both component totals and the feasible range of $\theta$ for that fixed inventory. As a separate feasibility control, if the same 1000-site geometry had only 30 B atoms total, would $\theta=0.625$ be allowed? Explain why zero excess at $\theta=x_b$ does not itself select equilibrium occupancy or prove a real boundary phase change.
