# Lesson 3 worksheet — count, label, conserve

Meetings 08–09. Paper route is complete: use tokens or written A/B marks, pencil,
this sheet and optional calculator. No laptop, chemistry database or numerical
solver is needed. Answers/hints are in the separate instructor guide. Record
whether your final explanation used a hint or partner support.

## Reference card

nA,nB: amounts of A/B atoms in mol; n=nA+nB. xB=nB/n within one homogeneous
phase; zB is that fraction for the whole sample. fα=nα/n is the fraction of
**all atoms in phase α**, not the B fraction within α. xBα is the fraction of
**α's atoms that are B**. Superscripts name phases, not powers. m=nM; for mass
exercises only MA=20 and MB=80 g/mol (invented). All fractions are dimensionless.
A token means one drawn atom; scaling to moles preserves the ratio, not its count.

## A1 · Twenty tokens, then a macroscopic amount

Worked: 15 A + 5 B tokens gives zB=0.25. Draw 12 A + 8 B and calculate both
fractions. Now describe 2 mol of atoms with those same fractions: find nA and nB.
Does that mean your 20 drawn atoms literally became 2 mol? Explain the scaling.

## B1 · Two boxes, same component inventory

Use 15 A + 5 B tokens. Put 9 A + 1 B in α, and the remaining 6 A + 4 B in β.
Find zB, fβ and xBβ, naming each denominator. On a one-mole total basis calculate
nAα,nBα,nAβ,nBβ and both phase masses. Compare β's mole phase fraction with its
mass phase fraction. Does a higher B concentration imply more atoms in that box?

## B3 · Detect a total-balanced but component-unbalanced report

For n=2 mol and zB=0.3 compare candidates:

| Candidate | nα, mol | nβ, mol | xBα | xBβ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I | 1 | 1 | 0.1 | 0.5 |
| II | 1 | 1 | 0.1 | 0.4 |

For each calculate total A and total B. A program reports low G for II and a
successful exit code. May we accept II as equilibrium of the stated closed
sample? Does the valid balance in I prove I is equilibrium?

## B4 · Determine an amount from supplied compositions

Worked: zB=0.25, xBα=0.1, xBβ=0.4 implies 0.25=0.1+0.3fβ, so fβ=0.5.
Try zB=0.3 with xBα=0.1, xBβ=0.6. Solve by substitution, then check both
component balances on a 5 mol basis. If zB becomes 0.8 with the same phase
compositions, why must the resulting fractions be rejected? Do not clip them.
Optional discussion: both phase compositions equal zB=0.3; can balance choose fβ?

## B5 · Fresh exit for meeting 09

A 4 mol sample has zB=0.35. Proposed phase compositions are xBα=0.2 and xBβ=0.5.
Find fα/fβ, nα/nβ, the A/B inventory of each phase and both conserved totals.
Explain the difference between fβ and xBβ even if their numerical values happen
to coincide in this example. What extra information would be needed to establish
that the proposed split is thermodynamic equilibrium? Name a missing quantity.
