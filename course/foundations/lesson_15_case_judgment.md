inputs. Use the
[worksheet](lesson_15_worksheet.md) and keep the

**Exit goal:** state the proposed material observable and compatible physical

GAP decision, and name the smallest eligible evidence packet that could move
the case forward. A precise stop is a useful research result; it is not a

## 15A — hold one question still while inspecting evidence

The provisional SFB 1394 teaching question is: for **one specified** Ni–Cu
grain boundary, how would Cu Gibbsian excess $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Cu}}$ in mol/m²
depend on a verified bulk reservoir state, and what evidence would be needed
before comparing two structural states? The provisional case
proposes a Ni [111] tilt boundary, but does not fix its plane,
misorientation, atom/site inventory, area, mechanical conditions, excess
convention, temperature/composition domain or matched energy data. The
[Lesson 10 audit](lesson_10_source_audit.md) records a separate missing
eligible Ni–Cu bulk model. The source checks behind the table are those dated
in Lesson 10; external sites were not rechecked for this lesson.

| Supplied record from the Lesson 10 audit | Supports now | Does not supply |
|---|---|---|
| SFB B06 project description | Ni–X research motivation and a pure-Ni [111] direction | A matched Ni–Cu boundary structure or measured $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Cu}}$ |
| Mey Cu–Ni reassessment citation | A bulk assessment **literature lead** | Exact rights-cleared, versioned runnable TDB with audited terms and independent result |
| SGTE pure-element descriptions | A restricted-use unary source role | Permission to combine/redistribute a Ni–Cu course model or a complete binary model |
| Fischer Cu–Ni/NIST entry | An atomistic segregation/potential lead for $\Sigma5$/$\Sigma3$ geometries | The proposed [111] Ni geometry, a CALPHAD TDB, or accepted excess/state energies for this case |
| Invented A/B models of Lessons 4–9 and 11–14 | A checked method for model bookkeeping and compatible comparisons in declared domains | A Ni–Cu parameter, material prediction or measured interface phase |

Read the last row carefully: no amount of checking the synthetic calculations
can turn invented $\delta$ or $\eta$ into Ni–Cu data.

### Worked source-to-claim example

Proposed sentence: “The NIST Cu–Ni potential validates Cu excess at the SFB
[111] Ni–Cu boundary.” Split it into links before accepting it:

1. **Identity:** the recorded NIST entry describes a Cu–Ni atomistic
   potential and a study of $\Sigma5$/$\Sigma3$ boundaries.
2. **Geometry:** the proposed [111] tilt boundary lacks a fixed plane,
   misorientation and matched state packet. Different named geometries cannot
   be equated from alloy labels alone.
3. **Quantity and model:** an EAM implementation is not a bulk CALPHAD
   Gibbs-energy assessment and does not by itself give compatible interface
   free energies or a Gibbsian excess for the proposed cell.
4. **Rights and independent check:** hosting/access and a DOI do not settle
   redistribution rights or a held-out comparison in the target domain.

The strongest current sentence is: “Fischer/NIST is an atomistic Cu–Ni
source lead for separately assessed boundary geometries; the proposed
Ni–Cu [111] excess case remains GAP.” A source lead is worth recording
without treating it as a failed numerical solver.

## 15B — choose the potential and evidence before a run

For a future **open reservoir** case, fixed $T,p$, bulk
$\mu_{\mathrm{Ni}}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{Cu}}$, boundary geometry and compatible
mechanical conditions would be declared first. The provisional two-
equivalent-boundary expression subtracts both component reservoirs:

$$\gamma_s=\frac{G_{\mathrm{cell},s}-\mu_{\mathrm{Ni}}N_{\mathrm{Ni},s}
-\mu_{\mathrm{Cu}}N_{\mathrm{Cu},s}}{2A}.$$

Here $G_{\rm cell}$ is J, $N_i$ is **mol of atoms** if $\mu_i$ is J/mol,
and $2A$ is m² only for two equivalent boundaries with no other surfaces.
The applicable interface excess and mechanical terms must be fixed for the
actual case; this sketch is not a universal stressed-interface law and has
**no values to evaluate**. A finite **closed** cell instead conserves Ni and
Cu separately and compares compatible constrained total energies. An open
grand potential and a closed total Gibbs energy cannot be subtracted.

The research decision sequence is:

| Step | Evidence required | Current decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — select exact case | Observable/reference, eligible source and exact proposed geometry with rights | **GAP**; Ni–Cu is editorially provisional |
| 2 — check bulk model | Exact complete TDB, unary/reference/magnetic/phase/domain semantics, stable reservoir result, independent numerical and held-out checks | **NOT ACCEPTED**; no real $\mu_i$ may be reported |
| 3 — check defect input | Matched geometry, state(s), area/sites/atoms, free-energy or excess convention, mechanical conditions, uncertainty, rights and independent evidence | **NOT ACCEPTED**; no real $\Gamma$ or state energies may be reported |
| 4 — bounded case | Separate physical/resource/run packet, compatible coupling, conservation, held-out comparison and independent result acceptance | **BLOCKED** until steps 1–3; no calculation launched here |

If **one** boundary state becomes evidenced, a conditional segregation
question may become eligible after steps 1–3 and a bounded, checked calculation.
A two-state transition question additionally needs **two matched structural
state free-energy functions** under one thermodynamic/mechanical reference,
uncertainty and appropriate interface coexistence analysis. A single-state
segregation curve, a numerical jump or hysteresis alone does not establish
an equilibrium interface phase transition. Equilibrium ordering also does
not establish a kinetic path, processing speed, strength or ductility.

“**Question:** Cu excess at one specified Ni–Cu [111] boundary versus a
