# Instructor guide — composition and component balances

Meetings 08–09. Use [reading](../foundations/lesson_03_composition.md) and
[worksheet](../foundations/lesson_03_worksheet.md). Timings are planned; they have not been observed with learners.
Preparation: paper tokens labelled
A/B or drawn marks, scratch paper, worksheet, separate hints/answers. No colour,
internet, code or prior chemical-potential vocabulary is required.

Two 90-minute meetings, each 85 instruction/practice + 5 break. If the inverse mass conversion or box notation stalls, use
one worked row and another supported attempt; defer optional equal-composition
discussion, preserve break/practice/exit and record incomplete concepts. Provide
additional support before ideal mixing; do not make unbudgeted homework repair
a prerequisite without recording the delivery change.

## Meeting 08 answers and detecting explanations

A1: xA=0.6, xB=0.4. For 2 mol, nA=1.2 and nB=0.8 mol. The scaled sample
contains 2NA atoms, not twenty; only its ratio is represented by the tokens.

| A2 nA, mol | nB, mol | n, mol | xB | nB/nA | B at.% |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 1 | 4 | 1/4 | 1/3 | 25 |
| 0.6 | 0.4 | 1 | 2/5 | 2/3 | 40 |
| 2 | 3 | 5 | 3/5 | 3/2 | 60 |

xB=30 is outside [0,1]. If the intended label was 30 at.% B, xB=0.30; without
a basis, the percentage alone is ambiguous. Do not silently assume mass percent.

| A3 nA, mol | nB, mol | mA, g | mB, g | xB | wB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 1 | 60 | 80 | 1/4 | 4/7 |
| 1 | 1 | 20 | 80 | 1/2 | 4/5 |
| 2 | 0.5 | 40 | 40 | 1/5 | 1/2 |

Inverse: 30 g A →1.5 mol; 40 g B →0.5 mol. xB=1/4; wB=4/7.
A4: equal 20 g gives 1 mol A and 0.25 mol B, so xB=1/5. Equal one-mole
amounts give 20/80 g, so wB=4/5. Density/geometry information would be needed
for volume/image interpretation; an atom or mass fraction is not an area fraction.

## Meeting 09 answers and detecting explanations

B1: zB=1/4, fβ=1/2, xBβ=2/5. On the one-mole basis α has 0.45 mol A,
0.05 mol B, mass 9+4=13 g; β has 0.30 mol A, 0.20 mol B, mass 6+16=22 g.
β's phase mass fraction=22/35, distinct from 1/2 and 2/5. A box can be B-richer
without holding more total atoms. Components can occur in both phases.

| B2 state | α tokens | β tokens | xBα | xBβ | fβ | zB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Before | 10 | 10 | 1/10 | 3/10 | 1/2 | 1/5 |
| After | 6 | 14 | 0 | 2/7 | 7/10 | 1/5 |

After moving 3 A+1 B, α contains 6 A+0 B and β 10 A+4 B. Whole counts remain
16 A+4 B. Phase amounts and compositions change; overall composition stays fixed.

B3 I: B=0.1+0.5=0.6 mol, A=0.9+0.5=1.4 mol, valid. II: B=0.1+0.4=0.5 mol,
A=0.9+0.6=1.5 mol, invalid. Success/low G cannot cure component imbalance.
I is feasible but not proven equilibrium: no energy comparison was supplied.

B4: fβ=(0.3−0.1)/(0.6−0.1)=0.4; fα=0.6. On 5 mol, nα=3 and nβ=2 mol.
α: A=2.7, B=0.3 mol; β: A=0.8, B=1.2 mol. Totals A=3.5 and B=1.5 mol.
At zB=0.8 the required fβ=1.4 and fα=−0.4 are invalid. Clipping changes the
inventory. If both compositions equal the overall 0.3, balance admits every
fraction; it cannot choose an amount. Equal phases at a different composition
cannot represent zB=0.3 for any amounts.

B5: fα=fβ=0.5, nα=nβ=2 mol. α: A=1.6, B=0.4 mol; β: A=1.0, B=1.0 mol.
Totals A=2.6 and B=1.4 mol. fβ=0.5 describes all atoms residing in β; xBβ=0.5
describes B atoms among β's atoms. The same number has a different denominator.
Need Gibbs energies of the candidate and competing feasible states, at specified
conditions, to establish equilibrium; supplied compositions alone do not do so.

## Readiness evidence

A4 and B5 require numerical work plus denominator/balance reasoning. Record
independent, supported or not-yet explanations without a universal pass score.
An annotated paper solution is sufficient for this lesson. Do not claim
independent understanding after revealing the answer or treating a lower energy
as evidence that conservation is optional.
