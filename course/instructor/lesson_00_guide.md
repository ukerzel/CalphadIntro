# Instructor guide — three meetings for Lesson 0

Times below are planned; they have not been observed with learners.
Use the [worksheet](../foundations/lesson_00_worksheet.md) and the existing
[reading](../foundations/lesson_00.md). Print the worksheet separately from
this answer key. No installation or internet connection is needed in class.

## Entry, teaching choices and readiness

Audience: materials researchers with no CALPHAD and little physical chemistry.
Start by asking for a specimen sketch and a negative-number comparison.
Define every symbol using the worksheet tables before placing it on the board.
Avoid derivatives and the phrase “disorder” as a substitute for entropy units
and the reversible-path relation. Explain a reversible process as an ideal
reference path, not a claim about a real furnace experiment.

The first two meetings use general definition exercises. The invented unary
phase model appears only in C4–C5, with its fixed pressure and temperature
domain. Do not attach the 300/400 K examples to those phase curves.
Teach “which conditions, which quantity, which amount?” before comparisons.
Definitions and constraints follow Lesson 0's primary sources; the worksheet
examples are original.

fit, stop and record which task remains; schedule more time before progressing.
Do not absorb the break or claim the packet was completed. C4/C5 can reveal
that a slower bridge is needed.

## Staged hints and worked answers

Give hint 1 first; offer hint 2 only after another attempt. Learners should
retain which hint they needed without being assigned a numerical performance
label. An answer after prompting is supported practice.

### Meeting 01

| Task | Hint 1 | Hint 2 |
|---|---|---|
| A2 | Does each arrow enter or leave the sample? | Put signs on q and work before adding; no heat does not imply no work. |
| A3 | Which quantities belong to a state? | Internal-energy change equals the signed transfers; final U also needs initial U. |
| A4 | Treat heat loss and work input separately. | Write −50 and +20 before taking their sum. |

A1: $100+(-30)=70$ J. The work arrow points outwards. The system is closed
to matter but exchanges energy.

A2(a): $q=80$, $w_{\mathrm{on}}=-10$, $\Delta U=70$ J.
A2(b): $q=-40$, $w_{\mathrm{on}}=15$, $\Delta U=-25$ J.
A2(c): $q=0$, $w_{\mathrm{on}}=25$, $\Delta U=25$ J.
Work transfer prevents isolation in (c). A1 and (a) have equal energy changes;
if their endpoints are the same states, the different separate heat/work
transfers are compatible with a state function.

A3: heat and work are process transfers, not two stores added to $U$.
The work done by the sample is negative work on it: $100-30=70$ J.
Final $U=U_{\mathrm{initial}}+70$ J needs the initial value and its reference.

A4: $\Delta U=-50+20=-30$ J. Closed conserves matter, not internal energy
when heat or work crosses. Look for an explanation of the two signs.

### Meeting 02

| Task | Hint 1 | Hint 2 |
|---|---|---|
| B2 | First separate a state definition from a process change. | pΔV=2 J; expansion work is −2 J; ΔH=ΔU+2 J. |
| B3 | Heat removed has a negative sign. | Divide −900 J by 300 K; add the supplied system/surroundings changes separately. |
| B4 | The temperature multiplying entropy must be absolute. | Kelvin is required; the equality also requires a reversible constant-temperature path. |
| B5 | Repeat the H and entropy examples as separate calculations. | pV=3 J; the second quotient is 800/400. |

B1: $100000(0.00001)=1$ J, $H=999+1=1000$ J.
Separately, $\Delta S=600/300=2$ J/K on the stipulated reversible isotherm.

B2 state: $pV=2$ J, $H=1998+2=2000$ J.
B2 process: $w_{\mathrm{on}}=-2$ J, $\Delta U=50-2=48$ J,
$\Delta H=48+2=50$ J. Equality to heat uses the stated constant pressure,
closed amount and only pressure–volume work. Electrical work would enter the
energy account and invalidate simply equating $\Delta H$ with heat.

B3: $\Delta S=-900/300=-3$ J/K. The separate combined-system example gives
$-2+3=+1$ J/K. Its sample entropy decrease does not contradict the second law:
the isolated combination has a positive change. This check alone does not
specify a complete realizable physical process.

B4: use kelvin (27 °C corresponds to 300.15 K). Even with temperature corrected,
actual irreversible heat is not automatically the reversible heat in the
formula. Do not calculate a sample entropy change from insufficient information.

B5: $pV=3$ J, $H=1500$ J; separately $\Delta S=2$ J/K.
H is a state function defined as U+pV, while heat is energy crossing a boundary
along a process. Ask for this distinction, not just the word “enthalpy.”

### Meeting 03

| Task | Hint 1 | Hint 2 |
|---|---|---|
| C2 | Calculate pV and TS in separate columns. | H=U+pV, F=U−TS, G=H−TS; then use the row matching fixed conditions. |
| C3 | Are the totals for the same amount? | Use G=ng; compare both at n=2 mol or compare their molar values. |
| C4 | Substitute T separately into each phase expression. | Solid: 1000−10×900; liquid: 7000−16×900. |
| C5 | Repeat C4 at the new temperature with the same parameters. | The liquid entropy makes its line fall faster; energy comparison does not supply a rate. |

C1: $H=1000$, $TS=3000$, $F=-2001$, $G=-2000$ J;
$G-F=1$ J equals $pV$.

C2: $H=2000$, $TS=2400$, $F=-402$, $G=-400$, $G-F=2$ J.
(a) fixed T,V,amounts: minimum F; (b) fixed T,p,amounts: minimum G;
(c) isolated fixed U,V,amounts: maximum S. All comparisons must use allowed
states of the specified simple bulk system.

C3(a): $G_X=2(-8000)=-16000$ J; $G_Y=3(-7400)=-22200$ J.
(b) At 2 mol, $G_Y=-14800$ J, so X is lower by 1200 J.
The original totals represented different conserved amounts.
(c) Need Helmholtz energies of admissible states at the common T,V,amounts;
G ordering alone does not answer that constrained problem.

C4: $g_S=-8000$, $g_L=-7400$ J/mol; solid is lower by 600 J/mol.
The liquid's larger s makes its −Ts contribution more negative per kelvin.
Enthalpy alone omits this contribution.

C5: $g_S=-10000$, $g_L=-10600$ J/mol; liquid is lower by 600 J/mol.
Both describe the same invented component at T=1100 K, p=100000 Pa and a
one-mole-of-atoms basis. The model lacks kinetic mechanisms/rate parameters,
so it predicts no transformation time.

## Handback and continuation

Record tasks attempted, where signs/units/conditions needed rescue, whether
learners could explain changed-number results, and any unfinished core work.
Readiness is an explanation plus arithmetic on A4, B5 and C5. Revisit missing
ideas before Lesson 1; keep C4/C5 as supported practice if required.
The optional depth is discussing alternative work sign conventions or how
state-function references work. It is not required for the next lesson.
